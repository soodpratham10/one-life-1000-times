        How it’s cast

        The model starts from Statistics Canada’s 2017–2019 life tables for your province and lowers future death rates the way the Office of the Chief Actuary assumes in the 32nd CPP Actuarial Report. Your answers adjust those rates using large published studies of smoking, activity, weight, alcohol and diet. Each simulated year, things can happen: a car crash, cancer, a heart attack or stroke, disability, marriage, divorce, a first child, retirement. Each one changes what comes later. Then the year ends in death, or the life carries on.

        Edmond Halley, the astronomer, published one of the first life tables in 1693, from the births and deaths of Breslau. This site follows the same idea with today’s data.

        What this is not

        • Not a prediction of when or how you will die. No model can do that for one person; this shows the range.
        • Not medical, financial or insurance advice.
        • Not an insurance price.

        Controls

        The model was first written in Python. The engine on this page is a JavaScript copy, tested against the Python results for a set of reference people. The core was also rebuilt separately in R and in Excel VBA, and they agree with Python to at least ten decimal places. A simulated population was compared with Canada’s actual deaths.
