Nobody can tell you how your life will go. Here are 1,000 ways it could.

Answer nine questions about yourself. The model then lives your life forward, year by year, from today to the end, using Canadian data and the methods actuaries use: health, accidents, family, work and how it ends. Then it does it again, a thousand times.

You see the realistic range of how things could go, and which of your choices actually move the odds.

About two minutes. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

        How it works

        What this is

        A simulation built on official Canadian data. It starts from Statistics Canada's 2017–2019 life tables for your province, lowers future death rates the way the Chief Actuary of Canada assumes for the Canada Pension Plan, and adjusts them for your answers using large published studies of smoking, activity, weight, alcohol and diet.

        Each simulated year, things can happen: a car crash, cancer, a heart attack or stroke, a disability, marriage, divorce, a first child, retirement. Each changes what comes later; a cancer diagnosis or a heart attack raises the risk of dying of it, a marriage lowers death rates a little. Then the year either ends in death or the life carries on.

        What this is not

        • Not a prediction of when or how you will die. No model can do that for one person; this shows the range.
        • Not medical, financial or insurance advice.
        • Not an insurance price.

        How it was checked

        The model was first written in Python. The version running on this page is a JavaScript copy, tested against the Python results for a set of reference people. The core was also rebuilt separately in R and in Excel VBA, and they agree with Python to at least ten decimal places. A simulated population was compared with Canada's actual deaths. Every assumption, its source and its weakness is written down.
